# M7 propagation and M8 interference and suppression: critical lesson review (task-45)

I read all five lessons, both module index files, all five quiz files and every bank item in B-007-001 to B-008-005. I checked the correct answers in `content/generated/basic-2025-07-15/questions.json`. I spot-read 21 of the explanations, choosing the hard items. I recomputed every number. Word counts are my own estimates by hand, because I had no word-count tool.

Overall: the content is mostly accurate and has good real-world anchors. There is one misleading physics explanation, one definition that is too broad, three exam questions the lessons never teach, and several that are only stated as answers. The modules also assume learners can map band names to frequencies, but nothing before M7/M8 in the recommended order teaches that. Verdict: **REQUEST CHANGES**.

## 2. Findings by priority

### High: accuracy errors and untaught exam content

**H1. `l-007-02…md:94`: wrong mechanism.** The lesson says "The slanted path spends more distance in the refracting region." That is not why oblique paths work above the critical frequency. At a glancing angle the wave only has to be bent through a small angle to come back down, so a layer can return frequencies above its vertical critical frequency (roughly MUF ≈ fc / cos of the angle of incidence). Path length in the layer is not the reason.
- Fix: replace with "a wave arriving at a shallow angle needs less bending to turn back, so the same layer returns a higher frequency." That supports B-007-006-004.

**H2. `l-007-02…md:76`: B-007-005-004 not taught.** The correct answer is "a measure of solar activity taken at a specific frequency". The lesson never says the solar flux index is measured at one fixed radio frequency.
- Fix: add that it is measured daily at 10.7 cm (2 800 MHz). This is also a natural Canadian hook: NRC measures it at the Dominion Radio Astrophysical Observatory near Penticton, BC. Verify the source before adding it.

**H3. `l-007-02…md:74–80`: B-007-005-006 not taught.** The lesson mentions only UV. "Particle radiation" never appears, and the distractors (solar wind, coronal mass ejections) are particle phenomena, so the learner has no way to choose.
- Fix: add one sentence. Day to day, both the Sun's electromagnetic radiation (UV, X-rays) and its particle radiation shape the ionosphere. Storms such as coronal mass ejections are occasional disturbances on top of that.

**H4. `l-007-02…md:155`: B-007-004-011 is answer-key only.** "Phase distortion" appears only in the summary.
- Fix: in the fading section (around line 45–47), explain that copies of a signal arriving with different delays smear its phase relationships. The result is distortion, not just a change in strength.

**H5. `l-007-03…md:44/84`: B-007-007-004 not taught.** The term "tropospheric wave" never appears. In that question the distractor "ground wave" is the trap.
- Fix: name it: "the part of the wave kept near the surface by bending in the lower atmosphere is called the tropospheric wave." Contrast it with ground wave, which follows and is affected by the surface itself.

**H6. `l-008-01…md:148` against `l-008-02…md:82`: definitions collide.** "A spurious emission is unwanted radio energy outside the intended signal" also covers out-of-band emission. That makes B-008-004-004 (correct answer: out-of-band; distractor: spurious) a coin-toss for anyone using this lesson's definition. EXP-B-008-003-003 repeats the same "outside the intended signal" wording.
- Fix: add a short contrast. Out-of-band emission is just outside the necessary bandwidth and comes from the modulation (splatter, key clicks). Spurious emissions are further away and include harmonics, parasitics and mixing products. State that harmonics are one kind of spurious emission. Then align EXP-B-008-003-003 with it.

**H7. `l-008-01…md:99`: "high-pass" is never named in L-008-01.** B-008-001-004 is in this lesson's own quiz (QZ-L-008-01-A), and B-008-001-010 is also covered here. Both are keyed "high-pass at the TV", but the term is first taught in L-008-02, which lists L-008-01 as its prerequisite.
- Fix: in line 99, name the filters: "a high-pass filter at the TV input", and "band-pass" for the VHF case. Point forward to L-008-02 for the details.

**H8. `l-008-01…md:82, 95, 190`: steers toward a wrong answer.** Three places present "filter or attenuator" as equal first cures. B-008-001-011, also in this lesson's quiz, keys "suitable filter at the receiver" and lists "RF attenuator" as a distractor. Line 101 has the correct priority, but the worked example and Try-it override it.
- Fix: make the filter the first cure everywhere. Present the attenuator as a fallback with a cost: it weakens the wanted signal too.

**H9. Module-level: missing prerequisite (band name to frequency).** These items need it: B-007-001-008, B-007-002-009, B-007-006-005/-009/-011, B-007-007-006, B-007-008-009/-011, B-008-004-001/-007/-010 and B-008-005-009.
- Facts the items rely on: 160 m ≈ 1.8 MHz, 80 m ≈ 3.5–4, 15 m ≈ 21, 10 m ≈ 28, 6 m ≈ 50, and the 31 m broadcast band sits between 40 m and 30 m.
- M6 gives only 2 m (`l-006-03…md:79`). The only band table is `l-001-04…md:59–63`, and M1 comes last in the README's recommended order.
- The explanations quietly supply the numbers (EXP-B-008-004-010, -004-007, B-007-006-005), so the lessons don't teach them.
- Fix: add a compact band table to L-007-01, labelled as reference and linked to 300 / f from M6, and point to it from L-008-02's harmonics section.

### Medium: learnability

**M1. `l-007-01…md:48, 127, 139`: "not a mirror" versus the bank's wording.** The lesson insists the ionosphere is not a mirror, but bank answers say "reflection by the ionosphere" (B-007-003-008) and "highest frequency a region can reflect" (B-007-006-001). Meanwhile B-007-003-007 needs real reflection at the Earth's surface.
- Fix: add one bridging sentence ("exam and operators often say 'reflect' loosely"). Add a four-row table for reflection, refraction, diffraction and scattering. It also serves B-007-003-009, B-007-008-010 and the "diffraction" distractor in B-007-001-004.

**M2. `l-007-01…md:50`: NVIS.** Say that NVIS only works below the critical frequency, because the wave goes nearly straight up, and that it usually uses a low horizontal antenna. The B-007-001-002 distractor "regional communications above the critical frequency" is otherwise untouched. Note that the critical frequency is only defined in L-007-02, so either forward-reference it or move the definition earlier.

**M3. `l-007-01…md:46–48`: ground wave and sky wave.**
- Add "the lower the frequency, the farther the ground wave; on lower HF in daytime it can reach around 200 km" (B-007-001-007/-008).
- Say that sky wave is also called the "ionospheric wave", which is the answer wording in B-007-001-005/-010.
- Remove the "HF" restriction on sky wave.

**M4. `l-007-02…md:119`: SID undefined.** Give the cause: a solar flare's X-rays suddenly raise D-region absorption on the sunlit side, and lower frequencies suffer most, which is why the cure is to go higher. Right now the cure is memorised without a reason.

**M5. `l-007-02…md:78–80`: missing numbers and reasons.**
- B-007-005-007: give the ceiling ("around 40 MHz or more at solar maximum, not 100 MHz").
- B-007-006-007: explain why 20 m stays open worldwide in daylight through the whole cycle (14 MHz usually sits below the daytime F2 MUF even at solar minimum).

**M6. `l-007-02…md:121`: beacon advice half-endorses distractors.** "Broadcast or time-signal stations are useful clues" points toward the WWVH-on-15-MHz and 31-metre-broadcast distractors of B-007-006-005. Say plainly that a beacon in the same band (10 m for 28 MHz) is the evidence. Signals on other frequencies do not prove a 28 MHz path.

**M7. `l-007-02…md:41–59`: chunking.** Split into "Absorption", "Multipath and selective fading" and "Polarization changes". Polarization has nothing to do with absorption.

**M8. `l-007-03…md:84` against `46–58`: 800 km versus 46.7 km.** A beginner who has just calculated 46.7 km will not believe "normal tropo ≈ 800 km". Explain that the horizon calculation is for handheld-to-repeater line of sight, while the 800 km figure assumes high, well-equipped weak-signal stations. Otherwise 800 km is just a number to memorise.

**M9. `l-007-03…md:106, 120`: why scatter shows up near the MUF.** B-007-008-002 and -007 are answer-key only. Add the reason: near the MUF the main sky wave is marginal and the skip zone is wide, so weak scattered energy becomes noticeable.

**M10. `l-008-01…md:74–76`: intermodulation outside the receiver.**
- "Both can be receiver-side effects" leaves the learner unready for B-008-001-006 (two commercial transmitters mixing outside your receiver), which is in the quiz. Add external non-linear junctions: corroded joints, metalwork, transmitter sites.
- Link "outside gain antenna → stronger off-channel signals into a handheld's broad front end → intermodulation" for B-008-001-007.

**M11. `l-008-01…md:138`: telephone filter.** Name the modular plug-in telephone RFI filter fitted at the telephone (B-008-002-002).

**M12. `l-008-02…md:78–86` and `l-008-01…md:123`: overmodulation.** "Overmodulation" appears only in the objectives (line 36), yet B-008-004-006 (splatter ← overmodulating) is in the quiz. Define it in the body and tie it to splatter. The L-008-01 misconception callout currently makes "overmodulated" read like a myth. Clarify that overmodulation is real, it just isn't the diagnosis for audio rectification.

**M13. L-008-01 is too big (≈3 000 words, 11 H2 sections).**
- Trim duplicates: Try-it 1 and 5 are the same doorbell cable; "Ferrite chokes and bypass capacitors" overlaps "Common-mode current"; the Common mistakes list repeats the body.
- Consider asking the coordinator to move the transmitter-side B-008-003 material (spurious emissions, key clicks, parasitics) next to the harmonics lesson. That would need approval for a coverage move.

**M14. All five lessons: Try-it answers are inline.** Each answer sits on the same line as its question (for example `l-007-01…md:123–127`, `l-008-01…md:188–192`), so there is no real retrieval before the quiz. Move the answers to a following "Check your answers" list that stays within the renderer subset.

**M15. Concept checks reward common sense, not physics.** QZ-L-007-01-A C1, QZ-L-007-02-A C3, QZ-L-007-03-A C1/C3 and all three in QZ-L-008-01-A have one obviously sensible, longest option against absolute-sounding distractors ("definitely", "guaranteed", "ignore").
- Replace at least one per quiz with a technical discrimination item. For L-008-01: "every band affected / one channel / whole dial" → overload / harmonic / audio rectification.
- Add one interleaved item per quiz, for example B-003-001 low-pass placement in M8, or 300 / f in M7.
- QZ-L-008-01-A has 1 of 8 official items from B-008-003; rebalance it.

**M16. "Worked example" labels.** The label is used for narrative vignettes (`l-007-01…md:89, 110`; `l-007-02…md:61`; `l-007-03…md:108`; `l-008-01…md:78, 160`). Meanwhile the one real M7 calculation (`l-007-03…md:46`) is labelled "In the real world". Relabel these, and add a templated hop-count example to L-007-01: a 5 000 km path is more than one F2 hop of about 4 000 km, so it needs multiple hops (B-007-003-005).

**M17. M8 has no triage summary.** Add a "symptom → likely cause → first cure" table at the start of L-008-02 or the end of L-008-01. Both summaries are currently fact lists rather than a framework a learner can use.

**M18. Visuals.** Skip geometry, the LUF–OWF–MUF window, common-mode versus differential current, and filter response curves are critical to understanding. Today they are briefs only. Prioritise these four illustrations.

## 4. Module notes

**M7 order (01 → 02 → 03) is sound:** first the paths, then what changes the ionosphere, then the VHF exceptions.
- Overlaps: tropospheric refraction and radio horizon appear in both L-007-01:44 and L-007-03:44. L-007-03 should refer back instead of repeating.
- Out of order: NVIS (L-007-01) depends on the critical frequency, which is only defined in L-007-02. HF scatter (B-007-008) sits in the VHF lesson but depends on skip-zone and MUF ideas. That works because of the prerequisites, but the lesson title undersells it.

**M8 order is inverted for its own questions.** L-008-01 covers questions whose answers are filter types that only L-008-02 teaches. L-008-01 is also an all-in-one lesson.
- Better split: receiver-side and device-side faults plus the filter vocabulary in one lesson; transmitter-side faults (harmonics, splatter, spurious emissions, key clicks, parasitics) in the other. That needs coordinator approval for coverage moves.
- Without a move: add a minimal filter glossary to L-008-01.

**Prerequisites from other modules are undeclared.**
- `prerequisites: []` on L-007-01 and L-008-01 hides real dependencies: M6's 300 / f and band names, M4/M5 non-linear devices and ferrite reactance, and M3's low-pass placement, key clicks and splatter (`l-003-01…md:50–80`, `l-003-05…md:48`).
- M8 re-teaches key clicks, splatter and low-pass placement without referring back to M3, which misses a cheap chance for spaced review.
- The band-name table exists only in M1, which comes last. This is the single biggest gap across modules (H9).

**No interleaving anywhere.** None of the five quizzes reuses an earlier lesson or module.

**Canadian specificity is thin.** Apart from the 60 Hz mains note and "licence privileges allow", M7/M8 could be any country's material. Penticton solar flux, CHU, high-latitude aurora effects and the Basic HF gate are all easy, accurate wins once verified.
