# Critical lesson review for task-45: M1 Regulations and Policies and M2 Operating Procedures

**Scope:** 10 lessons (L-001-01 to L-001-06 and L-002-01 to L-002-04), their quiz JSON files, and a sample of 19 explanations. I compared every claim with `amat_basic_quest_delim.txt` (the source of `questions.json`; the correct answer is always the first answer field), `docs/exam-facts.md` and basic physics. This was a read-only review; I edited nothing.

**About the numbers:** line numbers are counted by hand from the current files and may be off by 1. I couldn't run a word counter, so word counts are body-text estimates (±10%).

## Bottom line

- **Accuracy is mostly good.** Every number I recomputed is correct: 146.940 − 0.600 = 146.340 MHz; 20 A × 13.8 V = 276 W; 6 dB ≈ 4× power; 10 Ah / 2 A = 5 h; 2100 UTC − 5 h = 1600. Every regulatory row matches the bank's correct answer.
- **One lesson has real errors.** L-001-03 has a row whose plain reading inverts the rule. It also presents two pairs of facts that seem to contradict each other without explaining them.
- **L-001-04 has a framing problem.** It may lead learners to think a Basic-only holder can transmit below 30 MHz.
- **M1's reference style is the biggest learnability problem.** The tables mirror the answer key, one row per bank question, under the header "Fact established for this lesson". The "How these rules fit together" prose often describes the tables ("Offence rows protect trust", "The exposure rows describe human RF exposure") instead of teaching the rule.
- **Many exam items are taught only as answer-key strings, and one isn't taught at all.** Details are in the coverage section.
- **M2 is clearly better.** It is really a hybrid: concrete narrative, dialogues and small worked examples alongside reference tables.
- **The quizzes need a different presentation.** Every quiz is exhaustive (11 to 46 official items, `conceptChecks: []`), with no retrieval practice inside the lesson and no interleaving with earlier topics.

## 2. Findings by priority

### High: accuracy errors and untaught or misleading exam content

1. **L-001-03 line 70: the row reads backwards.** It says "Interfering with another station's transmissions | Permitted when *the station* is directly involved with a distress situation." The natural reading is that *the other* station is in distress, which would mean you may interfere with a distress station. The bank (B-001-011-010) says "When *your* station is directly involved". The explanation `b-001-011-010.json` repeats the ambiguity in its correct-answer line.
   - Fix: say "your own station", and add one sentence to the prose (lines 113–115) reconciling it with line 49 ("never acceptable"): the only exception is a station that is itself handling distress traffic.

2. **L-001-03 lines 62, 63, 65 and 67: three distress and frequency rules look contradictory and are never explained.** "Relief outside amateur bands is prohibited", "an operator in distress may use any means", and "you may respond to an unanswered distress call on an amateur band where you lack privileges" (B-001-011-002/003/005/007) all sit in one table.
   - Fix: add a short prose passage in "How these rules fit together" that separates three cases:
     - your own life is at risk: any means, no power limit;
     - answering an unanswered distress call on an amateur band: allowed;
     - organized relief work: amateur bands only.

3. **L-001-03, B-001-014-001 is not taught.** No row or prose covers the scenario of a non-certified friend on your station when a foreign station breaks in (correct answer: continue monitoring). The explanation cites L-001-03, but the lesson has nothing on it.
   - Fix: add a positive row under third-party traffic, for example "Supervising a non-certified guest: you keep monitoring and controlling the contact, including when a foreign station joins."

4. **L-001-04 line 143 (summary) and line 114: the 29.5 MHz repeater rule is framed as an exception to the below-30-MHz rule.** The summary says below-30-MHz frequencies need the extra qualification "except for the specific Basic-only repeater-output allowance". That suggests a Basic-only holder may transmit on 29.5–29.7 MHz. The bank establishes only the lowest repeater *output* a Basic-only holder may *use* (B-001-018-003/004). B-001-008-007 (Basic may operate cross-band repeaters) points to the usual case: you transmit into an input above 30 MHz and the repeater retransmits on 10 m.
   - Fix: verify against RBR-4 before rewording. Then rephrase as an output-frequency rule and say explicitly that the operator's own transmissions stay above 30 MHz unless they have Honours, Advanced or Morse.

5. **L-001-06, several items are answer-key only, with no "why".**
   - Line 62–63 (B-001-024-003/004): add one sentence of physics. An adult body is roughly the length of a half-wave at low VHF (a half-wave at 80 MHz is about 1.9 m), so it absorbs most strongly there, much as an antenna resonates. This also links back to M6.
   - Line 65 (B-001-024-006/008): define "controlled" (people aware of the exposure, such as operators and workers) and "uncontrolled" (the general public, who get lower limits).
   - Line 49 (B-001-023-005): state the default-process elements positively instead of only saying what's outside the process: written notice, addressing relevant concerns, and giving the public a chance to respond.
   - Lines 78–79 (B-001-025-003/004): explain the categories. Radio-sensitive equipment contains a receiver (a garage door opener does); associated equipment doesn't (stereo, home theatre).

6. **L-001-05 line 58 (B-001-021-003): no reason given for the offshore answer.** The reason is that 7 km offshore is still inside US territorial waters, which extend 12 nautical miles, about 22 km. Add that clause; without it the "Canadian rules apply offshore" distractor is attractive.

### Medium: learnability and smaller accuracy points

7. **L-001-03 line 54 (B-001-010-008/010): "reduced protection" understates the bank.** The bank says amateur operation is *not protected*. Reword, and say why: 902–928 MHz is an ISM band shared with licence-exempt devices. Line 53 (430–450 MHz) should name the reason too (amateurs are a secondary service there), linking back to line 51. Also fix the irrelevant bandwidth reasoning in the distractor notes of `b-001-010-008.json`.

8. **The broadcast rules contradict each other across lessons.** L-001-02 line 71 and B-001-007-002 say amateurs may *never* broadcast to the public. L-001-03 line 64 and B-001-011-004 say broadcasting safety-of-life communications *is* permissible. Neither lesson explains this. Add one sentence in L-001-03 lines 113–115.

9. **L-001-04 lines 112–114 (B-001-015-001/002): the rule is phrased vaguely.** "The operator and call sign governing that operation" should become something like "whoever has fewer qualifications sets the limits, whether the more-qualified person is the owner or the operator". That rule answers both bank items.

10. **L-001-04, undefined terms the exam depends on.** Line 105 (crystal control, B-001-019-001), line 106 (overmodulation: driving past 100% distorts the signal and widens it so it splatters onto neighbouring frequencies), and line 107 (what 100% modulation means). One-line plain definitions would turn three answer-key rows into one understandable idea: overmodulation causes splatter, which causes interference.

11. **L-001-04 lines 134–136: the 276 W example stops before the learner's obvious question** ("so is my 100 W radio illegal?"). Add a sentence: the final stage draws only part of the supply power, so a typical 100 W radio's final-stage input is well under 250 W. Keep the numbers qualitative unless a source is cited.

12. **L-001-04 lines 59–64: no memory aid for the band edges.** An accurate one exists: the lower edges double, 3.5 → 7 → 14 → 28 MHz, and 21 = 3 × 7. Avoid the "300 / metres" shortcut here. It is too rough for band edges (20 m works out to 15 MHz, which is one of the distractors).

13. **L-002-01 line 132 (B-002-003-009/010): sidebands are taught as two isolated facts.** Teach the convention, labelled as a practical convention: lower sideband below 10 MHz, upper sideband at 10 MHz and above. Separately, line 43 never explains *why* repeaters need an offset, even though the objective at line 36 promises it. The reason: a repeater can't listen on the same frequency it is transmitting on.

14. **L-002-02 lines 118–122: the S-unit example is incomplete.** Show the reasoning (6 dB = 3 dB + 3 dB = 2 × 2 = 4×) and add a reduction example with original numbers (for example 60 W → 15 W, avoiding the bank's 100 W and 25 W). The exam tests the quartering direction (B-002-006-007).

15. **L-002-03, wording and consistency.**
    - Lines 45, 54 and the summary: replace the repeated "narrow priority comparison" hedge with one plain sentence: "The bank calls the level just below distress an emergency message; it outranks safety messages."
    - Line 73: make the real-world script match the body and B-002-008-005 (call sign and situation in the break).

16. **M1 objectives and summaries are bureaucratic.** L-001-06 lines 37 and 106–108, L-001-05 line 35 and L-001-01 line 38 should be rewritten as concrete "you can…" statements. L-001-06's summary bullets currently carry no information.

17. **L-001-05 line 82: replace the memory trick with one that encodes the order.** For example: "In North America, your home call comes first, then the local area prefix. Under CEPT, the host country's prefix comes first." Point out the symmetry with L-001-03 lines 106 and 108: US visitors to Canada also use home call, then portable/mobile, then the local prefix, and CEPT is "Advanced" in both directions.

18. **README rule conformance.** L-001-01 line 94 and L-001-06 line 49 are negative rows that name the wrong option, contrary to the README rule ("positive rule statements"). For "NOT correct" bank items, state the true companion facts positively so the wrong option stands out by its absence.

## 4. Module-level notes

### Sequencing
The README order puts M2 seventh and M1 last. That correctly avoids the "regulations first" failure mode. Three problems remain:
- **Prerequisites stop at the module boundary.** L-001-01 and L-002-01 have `prerequisites: []`, and the other lessons only point to the previous lesson in the same module. L-001-04 depends on modulation types, PEP/ERP/EIRP, antenna gain and the final RF stage (M5, M6, M3). L-002-01 depends on the dummy load, tuner and sideband (M3, M6). L-002-02 depends on dB. Add cross-module prerequisites (this needs coordinator approval, because the teaching guide freezes front matter).
- **IDs don't match the order.** "M1" is taught last, which will confuse learners browsing the app.
- **Lesson load is lopsided.** L-001-03 has 46 items and 5 subsections; L-001-05 has 14. Moving subsection B-001-014 (third-party and visiting operators) to L-001-05 would give roughly 35 and 25 items, and put all the cross-border symmetry in one place. This is a coverage move, so it needs coordinator approval.

### Overlaps that aren't used
- **The distress thread** runs through L-001-01 (false MAYDAY penalties), L-001-03 (distress exceptions) and L-002-03 (procedure). None of them cross-reference each other. Since M2 is taught before M1, L-001-03 should recall L-002-03.
- **Repeaters** are defined twice (B-001-018-001 in L-001-04, B-002-001-002 in L-002-01).
- **Minimum power** appears twice (B-001-017-001, B-002-004-002).
- **SSB width vs SSB spacing:** L-001-04 line 78 gives SSB width as 2–3 kHz, and L-002-01 line 138 gives 3 kHz spacing. Linking them turns a number to memorize into a reason.

### Is the reference style working?
Reference style is honest and safe for arbitrary legal facts. As built, it fails the rubric's "not just the answer" test in M1, for four reasons:
1. The tables have one row per bank question, in bank order.
2. The prose talks about the tables rather than explaining the rules.
3. There is no retrieval practice between reading and a 33–46 item quiz.
4. The ban on misconception sections removed the very discriminations the exam tests: install vs operate, Florida vs offshore, US vs CEPT order, whose privileges apply.

These changes would teach better and still fit the README rules:
- **Organize around a few generating principles instead of bank order.** Each principle is one prose sentence followed by the rows it explains. Examples: "both sides authorized"; "fewer qualifications sets the limit"; "Advanced is needed to build, to install for others, and to run unattended or remote stations"; "amateur traffic is non-commercial, not broadcast and not secret"; "where you stand decides whose rules apply"; "emergency exceptions exist for life safety".
- **Add a short "Why" column to the reference tables.** It is original prose, and the README allows tables.
- **Build one qualification-by-privilege matrix spanning L-001-02 and L-001-04.** Rows: Basic, 80% or Honours, Morse, Advanced. This is the single most valuable change.
- **Add an "M1 numbers board" reference table:** 30 days, 48 h, $60, 5 wpm, 30 minutes, 30 MHz, 80%, 250/560/190 W, 1 000/2 250 W, 30/20/6 kHz, the 30-day comment period, and 48–300 MHz.
