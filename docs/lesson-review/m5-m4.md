# Critical review: M5 Basic Electronics and M4 Circuit Components (task-45)

**Scope:** 8 lessons (L-005-01 to L-005-05 and L-004-01 to L-004-03), their 8 quizzes, and a sample of the per-question explanations (about 20 files).

**How I checked:**
- Every lesson read in full.
- Every number recomputed.
- Every covered question checked against `content/sources/ised/basic-2025-07-15/questions/amat_basic_quest_delim.txt`, where the first answer is the correct one.

**Limits:**
- I couldn't read the ISED reference PDF with the tools I had. The lessons say "the reference sheet gives…" in several places (L-005-02:28,85; L-005-03:42,76; L-005-04:124; L-004-03:71). Those claims are **unverified**.
- Word counts are hand estimates (±10%), because I couldn't run a counter.

**Overall:** Accuracy is strong. I found no wrong exam answers and every calculation checks out. The weak points are:
- one question that is never taught;
- a few exam discriminators that are only taught as answer-key facts;
- "Try it" sections that give the answer in the same line as the prompt;
- no faded practice anywhere;
- weak concept checks in the M5 quizzes;
- missing visuals for lessons that depend on schematic symbols;
- one overloaded lesson, L-005-04.

M4 is the stronger module.

## 2. Findings by priority

### High: accuracy errors and untaught exam content

1. **L-005-05:173 (and transformer section 39–60): B-005-011-002 is never taught.**
   - The only mention is "Transformers appear in … impedance-matching networks." Nothing explains *why* a transformer changes impedance.
   - The explanation file (EXP-B-005-011-002) carries the whole idea.
   - Fix: add 2–3 sentences. AC impedance is E / I. A transformer gives each winding a different voltage and current, so each winding sees a different impedance.

2. **L-005-01:102–106: B-005-002-004 (copper vs aluminum) and B-005-002-002 (carbon) are only partly taught.**
   - Copper and aluminum are both listed as "good conductors" with no ranking. Line 106 says carbon should be "treated separately", which doesn't tell the learner whether carbon conducts.
   - Fix: give the order silver > copper > gold > aluminum. Say plainly that carbon conducts, but much worse than metals (that is why it's used in resistors), so it is neither a good conductor nor an insulator.

3. **L-005-04:64–67: B-005-008-004, -005 and -011 are only partly taught.**
   - "S-meter" and "S9" are never defined. M2, where S-meters are taught, comes much later in the recommended order.
   - No worked example shows the full chain: power ratio → dB → new S-meter reading. The S9+18 dB example starts from a dB change that is already given.
   - Fix: add a one-sentence S-meter definition. Add a worked example using numbers that aren't in the bank (for example, 80 W → 8 W is 1/10, which is −10 dB, so S9+25 becomes S9+15).

4. **L-005-04:70–110: the core dB method is never taught.**
   - The table gives +9 dB ≈ 8x and −30 dB ≈ 1/1000 as bare facts. That is answer-key teaching for B-005-008-009 and -011.
   - The worked examples use 7 dB ≈ 5x (line 83) and 5 dB ≈ 0.3 (line 94) with no derivation, so a learner can't reproduce them.
   - The "large power reduction" example (103–110) multiplies ratios and never mentions dB.
   - Fix: add the rule "dB values add when ratios multiply" (3+3+3 = 9 dB → 2×2×2 = 8x; 10−3 = 7 dB → 10/2 = 5x). Rewrite example 103 as a dB chain.

5. **L-005-05:112–122 and L-005-04:150–152: B-005-012-008 is only partly taught.**
   - The "Mylar sheets" distractor needs the learner to know that a dielectric between the plates raises capacitance. Nothing in the lessons says so (L-005-04 lists only plate area and spacing).
   - Nothing says that more L or more C lowers the resonant frequency.
   - Fix: add one sentence on dielectric material to L-005-04:150, and one sentence on the direction of change to L-005-05:112.

6. **L-005-05:46: the permanent-magnet claim is misleading.**
   - "Permanent magnets are usually made from … steel" is out of date. Most modern magnets are ferrite, alnico or neodymium.
   - Fix: say permanent magnets are made from ferromagnetic materials that hold magnetism, such as hardened steel. Copper, aluminum and brass can't be magnetized. This keeps B-005-011-010 correct without the wrong generalization.

### Medium: learnability

1. **"Try it" sections give the answer in the same line, in all 8 lessons.**
   - L-005-01:137 even says "Do these in your head before checking the answer", and then each line contains the answer.
   - Many items are statements, not questions: L-005-03:201,204,205; L-005-05:165–168; L-004-01:160–166; L-004-02:176–180; L-004-03:208–210.
   - The result is zero retrieval before the quiz.
   - Fix: write each item as a question and move the answers to a separate list after the questions.
   - Caveat: Swift and web tests pin L-005-01's block order, so update those tests if this changes.

2. **No faded or completion problems anywhere in scope.**
   - Fix: after the first full worked example in each calculation lesson, add one partly blank version (for example, "R = 12 V / [ ] A = [ ] ohms").

3. **Duplicated content.**
   - The LED worked examples are copied almost word for word between L-005-02:145–170 and L-004-01:132–156.
   - The 9 V / 3 A / 27 W charger appears in both L-005-01:94–98 and L-005-02:56–65.
   - L-005-01's Radio connection section repeats its own Try-it numbers.
   - Fix: replace L-004-01's LED block with a diode-specific example, such as the voltage drop through a reverse-polarity diode (13.8 V − ~0.7 V ≈ 13.1 V) or half-wave pulsating DC, and refer back to L-005-02 for the LED.

4. **L-005-04 is overloaded.** It covers 4 subsections, 44 bank questions, 7 objectives, and has a 45-minute estimate. See the module notes for a proposed split; that needs coordinator approval because it moves coverage.

5. **L-005-05:112–113: resonance is asserted with no "why".** "At that frequency, the circuit has a special response" is filler.
   - Fix: explain that in series, the L and C reactances cancel, leaving only resistance, so impedance is low and current is at its maximum. In parallel, energy swaps back and forth between L and C, so the circuit draws little current from the source and impedance is high.

6. **L-005-04:160–166: reactance has no intuition, and two questions are weakly taught.**
   - "Comparing" AC voltage with current is weaker than the ratio that B-005-010-005 asks for.
   - B-005-010-009 needs the learner to see that a bypass capacitor has *high reactance at audio*. The lesson only implies this ("when chosen correctly").
   - Fix: write "Z = E / I for AC", and add one sentence on why the capacitor has high reactance at audio. Optionally add the intuition that an inductor opposes *change*, so faster change means more opposition.

7. **L-005-05:149: accuracy vs precision (B-005-013-010).** Only accuracy is defined, and precision and resolution are the classic traps. Fix: add a short three-way contrast.

8. **L-005-03:164 and the scattered rule for B-005-006-004 / -005 / -010.**
   - "This can happen when the circuit applies voltage in a way that shares the load evenly" suggests sharing depends on circumstances. In fact, equal resistors share power equally whether they are in series or in parallel.
   - Fix: state one rule: N equal resistors, in series or in parallel, can dissipate N × the rating of one.

9. **Undefined terms and prerequisite leaps.**
   - "Forward biased" (L-004-01:96; needed for B-004-002-010): define it as the anode being more positive than the cathode.
   - L-004-01:160–161: "preselector", "mixer", "modulator", "speech processor".
   - L-004-02:47: "holes". L-004-02:59: "biased" and "active region".
   - L-005-02:189: "final RF stage" and the regulatory DC-input limit, two modules before M1.
   - L-005-04:154: "after rectification", one module before diodes are taught.
   - Fix: either define each in a clause or remove it.

10. **Wrong prerequisites in front matter.**
    - L-004-01:17 is `[]`. It should list L-005-02 and L-005-04, because the lesson uses Ohm's law, dB and AC.
    - L-004-03:16–17 lists L-004-02 (transistors), which has nothing to do with colour codes. It should list L-005-01 (kilohms) and L-005-03 (power ratings).

11. **Weak M5 concept checks and quiz balance.**
    - Giveaway distractors:
      - QZ-L-005-01 C2 ("must be made of metal") and C3 ("changes MHz into kHz").
      - QZ-L-005-04 C3 ("voltmeter placed in series", "wire labelled as an insulator").
      - QZ-L-005-05 C2 ("capacitor is no longer part of the circuit") and C3 ("parallel with the supply fuse only").
    - Copies of lesson examples (recognition, not transfer):
      - QZ-L-005-02 C2 uses Try it #1's numbers (7.5 V, 0.015 A).
      - QZ-L-005-04 C2 repeats the 33 µF worked example.
      - QZ-L-005-05 C1 repeats the 800/120/240 V worked example.
    - QZ-L-005-03 C3 is a near-restatement of B-005-006-001.
    - Concept-check feedback is one sentence with no diagnosis of the wrong choices.
    - No quiz includes an item from an earlier lesson.
    - The official-question picks skip the hardest items:
      - QZ-L-005-04 has no S-meter (B-005-008-004/005/011) or bypass/choke (B-005-010-007..010) items.
      - QZ-L-005-03 has no power-sharing items (B-005-006-004/005/010).
      - QZ-L-005-05 has no B-005-011-002 or meter-resistance items (B-005-013-005/006).
    - The M4 concept checks are noticeably better: applied and plausible (JFET bias change, 1 080-ohm tolerance, the multiplier swap).

12. **Visuals (cross-cutting).**
    - The "Illustration brief" renders as a placeholder box with the *artist instructions* ("Draw a simple ink-brush…") shown to learners (apps/web/src/components/MarkdownContent.tsx:65–73).
    - L-004-02:71–78 (NPN arrow trick), L-004-02:176 (Try it 1) and L-004-01:100–106 (cathode-bar trick) all depend on symbols the learner never sees.
    - Fix: produce the illustrations, at least for the symbol-dependent lessons. Until then, hide the artist brief from learners and describe each symbol fully in text.

13. **L-005-01:58: weak mnemonic.** "Great **M**ighty Kings Balance **M**any **M**icro Picnics" uses three M words, so the first letter can't tell mega, milli and micro apart. It also differs from the vetted version in the teaching guide. Fix: use the vetted form, or one that distinguishes the M prefixes.

14. **Summaries are fact lists, not synthesis.** L-005-04:206–216 has 9 bullets; L-005-05:193–202 has 8. Fix: cut each to 3–4 bullets that link the ideas (for example, "reactance depends on frequency → chokes and bypasses → resonance where X_L = X_C").

15. **L-005-03 tangents and the 72-ohm load.**
    - The battery packs (140–148) belong to B-003-016, and the loaded voltage divider (150–158) isn't in the bank.
    - The 72-ohm "dummy load" (190) undercuts the 50-ohm idea in the Radio connection section.
    - Fix: move or trim the tangents, and relabel the 72-ohm load as a generic test load rather than a transmitter dummy load. Watch the originality checker if you add the number 50 to a block.

## 4. Module notes

**Sequencing within M5.** L-005-01 → 02 → 03 is a good concrete-to-abstract ladder. L-005-04 and L-005-05 break it.
- L-005-04 joins two unrelated topics: AC terms plus dB, and L/C combinations plus reactance.
- Resonance, the natural payoff of reactance, is pushed into L-005-05 next to transformers and meters.
- Proposed split (coverage moves need coordinator approval):
  - (a) AC and dB: B-005-007 and -008.
  - (b) Capacitors, inductors, reactance and resonance: B-005-009, -010 and -012.
  - (c) Transformers and meters: B-005-011 and -013.
- Alternatively, move meters (B-005-013) right after L-005-03. Voltmeter-in-parallel and ammeter-in-series are a direct application of series and parallel.

**Sequencing within M4.** The colour-code lesson (L-004-03) is the simplest and about passive parts, yet it sits after the transistors and tubes lesson only because of subsection numbering. Teach it first in M4, or link it straight from L-005-03, which already introduces resistor size and power rating (L-005-03:160–185).

**Cross-module prerequisites.**
- M5 is first in the recommended order, so it can't assume terms from later modules:
  - S-meter (M2);
  - final RF stage and the DC-input limit (M1);
  - rectification (M4);
  - feedline and dummy load (M6/M3).
- M4 depends on M5 (Ohm's law, dB, AC), but L-004-01's front matter lists no prerequisites.
- Capacitors and inductors as components live in M5 (B-005-009/010), not M4. Given the bank structure that's fine, but the M4 index line ("parts used in … filters") sets an expectation that M4 doesn't meet.

**Overlaps.**
- The LED series-resistor examples are copied (L-005-02 and L-004-01).
- The charger example appears twice (L-005-01 and L-005-02).
- Supply and transformer sizing appears in both L-005-03:167–175 and L-005-05.
- Resistor power rating appears in L-005-03 and L-004-03. Repeating it is a fine spiral, but L-004-03 should refer back to L-005-03 rather than re-teach it.

**Across both modules.**
- No interleaving: no quiz pulls in an item from an earlier lesson.
- No faded practice anywhere.
- Every "Try it" gives its answer in the same line.
- No diagrams render; only artist briefs, which learners can see.

Fixing these four things would lift scores on criteria 6, 9 and 10 for all eight lessons, more than any single lesson rewrite would.
