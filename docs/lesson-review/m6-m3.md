# Critical lesson review: M6 feedlines and antennas, and M3 station, practice and safety

I read all 13 lessons in full, all 13 quizzes, and both module indexes. I also checked all 339 bank questions in scope (B-006: 140, B-003: 199) against `questions.json`, using the correct answer for each. Where a gap showed up, I read the matching explanations. I recomputed every number in the lessons and quizzes.

I couldn't run `wc`, so the word counts are estimates of the lesson text without front matter, counted section by section (±10%).

**Overall:** M3 is clearly the stronger module. Its coverage is nearly complete, its real-world examples are excellent, and its concept checks test transfer to new situations. M6 has one statement that contradicts the bank. It also leaves about 15 tested ideas untaught, a few of them in its own quizzes. It has no diagrams at all, and its concept checks can be answered by eliminating silly options.

Two problems affect all 13 lessons:
- **No real retrieval practice.** Every "Try it" answer sits on the same line as its question.
- **No interleaving.** No quiz includes an item from an earlier lesson.

### Justifications that apply to every lesson in a module

- **C6, all lessons:** the Given/Find/Formula format is used correctly, but no lesson has a partly worked practice problem before the quiz. Several sections labelled "worked example" are really stories. Examples: L-006-01:94, L-006-02:84, L-003-03:79 and :85, L-003-02:70.
- **C9 for M6 (2):** antennas are spatial, but M6 has no diagram at all, only `## Illustration brief` placeholders. Radiation patterns, polarization, standing waves and Yagi layout are all described in words only.
- **C9 for M3 (2–3):** the text block diagrams help where they exist (L-003-01, -02, -03). L-003-04, -05, -07 and -08 have no diagrams.
- **C10, all lessons:** each quiz has 7–8 official items and 3 concept checks, with no item from an earlier lesson.
  - M6 concept checks are mostly giveaways, for example "It becomes coaxial cable when it touches metal" (QZ-L-006-01-A-C2), "changes metres into megahertz" (-04-C3), and "Equally nowhere because a dipole does not radiate" (-05-C1).
  - M3 checks are genuinely diagnostic. However, 5 of the 24 use negative "NOT" stems, and QZ-L-003-01-A-C1's keyed option is absurd.
- **C11 (3):** explanations for official questions are good and give a note for each distractor. Each concept check has only one explanation sentence and nothing on why each wrong option is wrong.
- **C14 (4; 3 where noted):** headings are in order, tables have header rows, formulas are text, and illustration briefs have alt text. The receiver and transmitter text diagrams (L-003-01:113–117; L-003-02:44–49, 78–81, 104–108) use `^` characters lined up by column to show side inputs. That breaks on narrow screens and doesn't read well in a screen reader.
- **Summaries, all lessons:** they restate bullets rather than tie ideas together. No summary connects the lesson's ideas into one cause-and-effect story.

## 2. Per-lesson review

### L-006-01 Feedline basics and types

**Accuracy.** The cut-coax impedance example and the connector table are correct.
- Line 124 ("Putting a balun at the radio end…") contradicts B-006-002-010, where the correct answer is a balun between an unbalanced tuner and window line. It also clashes with B-006-002-008. This is the most serious error in the review.
- Line 44 lists "conductor spacing, conductor diameter, and dielectric" as what sets Z0. It never says **ratio**, which is what B-006-001-004 and B-006-001-011 test.

**Coverage (33 questions).**
- Not taught: velocity factor (B-006-001-007, and it is **in the quiz**), foam vs solid dielectric (B-006-003-002, B-006-003-011), solid shield (B-006-003-009), and how input impedance varies with line length (B-006-001-009).
- Weak: B-006-001-005 (buried coax appears only in Try it #5) and B-006-001-006 ("suits the line" at :46 never says load = Z0).

**Score notes.**
- C2 = 2: 5 questions untaught, 1 contradicted.
- C3 = 3: "common-mode" is used at :17, :110 and :140 but never defined, and the lesson doesn't build on M5's treatment of impedance.
- C8 = 3: the Common mistakes list includes the wrong statement at :124.
- C10 = 2: the quiz tests velocity factor, which the lesson never teaches. Check C1 nearly repeats Try it #1.
- C12 = 1: no memory aid anywhere.

**Learnability.**
- The concrete opening (cut coax, TV coax in walls) is good.
- The objectives promise "choose feedline based on … common-mode concerns" but the lesson never explains common-mode current.
- There's no "inside the cable" section, which is exactly where velocity factor, foam, and shield type belong.

### L-006-02 Loss, SWR, and matching

**Accuracy.** All numbers check: 1.4 dB loss; reflection coefficients 0.2, 0.333 and 0.5 giving 4%, 11% and 25%; ratio 240/60 = 4. One worked example (:109–117) uses a "60 ohm test cable" and a "folded antenna near 240 ohms". Neither is realistic, and folded dipoles are about 300 Ω (B-006-006-011). These numbers seem chosen to get past the originality checker, and they plant a false real-world anchor.

**Coverage (32 questions).**
- Not taught: B-006-006-007 (transformers can match over a wide bandwidth) and B-006-006-009 (once matched, line length doesn't matter).
- Weak:
  - RG-58 and RG-213 are never named, so B-006-004-001 (**quizzed**) and B-006-004-006 are hard to answer.
  - "Standing wave" is never explained (B-006-005-007, -008).
  - Why high SWR raises line loss is stated but not explained (B-006-005-005, quizzed).
  - "Less than 1.5:1 = fairly good" only appears implicitly in the table (B-006-005-002).
  - That a tuner adds inductive or capacitive reactance is only implied by :102 (B-006-006-006).

**Score notes.**
- C3 = 3: "reflection coefficient" appears at :74 without explanation, and skin effect (:45) is described but not named, even though a distractor in B-006-004-010 uses the term.
- C8 = 4: the tuner misconception callout is good.

**Learnability.**
- The lesson is called SWR but never says what a standing wave physically is. That is the missing "why" behind three things: why mismatch raises loss, why open-wire line tolerates high SWR (B-006-004-002), and why matching at the antenna helps.
- The reflected-power table is useful, but it appears before any physical picture, so it reads as arithmetic with no meaning attached.

## 3. Findings by priority

### High: accuracy errors and untaught exam content

1. **L-006-01:124.** This Common mistakes bullet contradicts B-006-002-010 and B-006-002-008.
   - Fix: replace it with the general rule, "a balun goes wherever balanced meets unbalanced." For a coax-fed dipole that is the feed point; when window line is fed from an unbalanced tuner, it is the tuner output.

2. **L-006-01 (no section; quiz includes B-006-001-007).** Velocity factor, foam vs solid dielectric and shield types are never taught (B-006-001-007, B-006-003-002, B-006-003-009, B-006-003-011).
   - Fix: add a short "Inside the coax" section. Cover: the dielectric sets the velocity factor (a fraction of light speed); foam has lower loss, but the centre conductor can move when the cable is tightly wound, which is why chokes use solid dielectric; a solid shield beats double braid, which beats single braid, which beats foil.

3. **L-006-01:44, :46.** Z0 is never explained as a ratio, and the effect of line length on impedance is untaught (B-006-001-004, B-006-001-009, B-006-001-011, B-006-001-006, B-006-006-009).
   - Fix: say "the ratio of shield diameter to centre-conductor diameter (coax), or centre spacing to wire diameter (open-wire)". Add two sentences: with a matched load, line length doesn't change what the radio sees; with a mismatch, the impedance at the radio end depends on line length. Replace "suits the line" with "a load equal to Z0 absorbs all the energy."

4. **L-006-02:66–70.** Standing waves are never explained, and the "why" behind B-006-005-005, -007, -008 and B-006-004-002 is missing.
   - Fix: add a 3–4 sentence physical picture. Forward and reflected waves add and cancel in fixed places, leaving voltage and current peaks along the line. The current peaks raise conductor and dielectric loss. That is why low-loss open-wire line tolerates high SWR.

5. **L-006-02:45–47.** RG-58 and RG-213 are never named, so B-006-004-001 (quizzed) and B-006-004-006 depend on knowledge the lesson doesn't give.
   - Fix: one sentence: "RG-58 is thin, lossy coax; RG-213 is thicker and lower-loss." Add B-006-006-007 (transformers can be designed to match over a wide bandwidth) in the matching section at :102.

6. **L-006-03:62 and :46.** B-006-008-004 (300 000 km/s) and B-006-007-010 (ground wave) are untaught.
   - Fix: derive 300/f from 300 000 km/s. Add one sentence defining ground wave as a wave travelling along the Earth's surface that keeps vertical polarization, with a forward pointer to M7.

7. **L-006-04:72, :89, :140.** B-006-011-010 (0.20 λ) is deliberately withheld, and B-006-011-006, B-006-010-005 and B-006-011-011 are absent or appear only in Try it. The stacking rationale at :140 is misleading.
   - Fix: state the 0.2 λ compromise and that wider spacing raises gain. State that a quarter-wave vertical radiates equally in all horizontal directions. Move stacking into the body as "+3 dB because the pattern is concentrated, not because more power is produced."

8. **L-003-08:122–130 (quizzed).** B-003-020-010 is untaught.
   - Fix: add "Before any antenna repair, turn off the transmitter and disconnect the feedline" to the towers paragraph, and contrast it with grounding, which does not remove an RF source.

### Medium: learnability and misleading framing

9. **All 13 lessons, `## Try it` (for example L-006-01:107–111 and L-003-05:146–150).** Answers sit on the same line as each question, so there is no retrieval effort.
   - Fix: list the questions alone, then add a separate "Check your answers" list or subheading after them, using only syntax the renderers already support.

10. **L-006-05:77.** "Require a stronger support" mirrors the B-006-013-006 distractor.
    - Fix: make weather vulnerability (ice and wind on loops and spreaders) the stated disadvantage, and drop the support claim.

11. **L-006-03:93 and L-006-05:94.** The "practical length is shorter" rule conflicts with 306/f loops and leads to the 41.96 m distractor (B-006-013-009).
    - Fix: add a `### Misconception:` callout: dipoles are cut shorter than free space (143 < 150), full-wave loops longer (306 > 300).

12. **L-003-03:93 and QZ-L-003-03-A-C3, option 4.** These endorse bleeder discharge, which conflicts with B-003-019-009.
    - Fix: present the insulated shorting stick with a series resistor, followed by a meter check, as *the* method, with bleeders only as a backup that may have failed. Reword the C3 option.

13. **L-003-02:46–53.** The keyed stage isn't named (B-003-004-004).
    - Fix: "The oscillator runs continuously; the key turns the carrier amplitude on and off in the driver or amplifier stage. Keying the oscillator would cause chirp."

14. **L-003-05:46, :56.** Chirp cures are listed without priority (B-003-011-002).
    - Fix: lead with "steady supply voltage under changing load" as the cure, and refer back to L-003-02 for the buffer's load-isolation role.

15. **L-003-07:139–166, plus EXP-B-003-017-009.** Wire gauge numbering is not taught.
    - Fix: add "a higher gauge number means thinner wire; pick the thinnest gauge whose round-trip drop meets the limit." In the explanation, reword the No. 8 note so it doesn't say "minimum wire gauge" ambiguously.

16. **All M6 lessons.** There are no visuals.
    - Fix: produce the illustration briefs, and add briefs for a standing-wave picture and a vertical's horizontal pattern. Until then, describe each pattern from above and from the side in text.

17. **M6 quizzes.**
    - Giveaway distractors: QZ-L-006-01-A-C2, -02-A-C2, -04-A-C3, -05-A-C1.
    - Duplicates of Try it items: QZ-L-006-04-A-C2 = L-006-04:141, and QZ-L-006-01-A-C1 is close to L-006-01:107.
    - Fix: rewrite distractors around real misconceptions, for example "a tuner reduces feedline loss", or "18 m" for the 60 MHz wavelength item (from multiplying instead of dividing). Add one item from an earlier lesson to every quiz in both modules.

18. **Chunking.** L-003-08 (~2 300 words, 4 subsections), L-003-05 (~2 050) and L-003-07 (~2 000) are long, and L-003-04 pairs unrelated topics.
    - Fix: split L-003-08 into B-003-018/019 (electrical safety) and B-003-020/021 (lightning, towers, RF exposure). Shorten L-003-04's Yagi section to a recap that points to L-006-04.

19. **Undefined terms.** "common-mode" (L-006-01:17, :110, :140), "standing wave" and "reflection coefficient" (L-006-02:68, :74), skin effect not named (L-006-02:45), "full quieting" (L-003-05:140), "skirts" (L-003-04:87), "fixed pads" (L-003-03:53), "grid" (L-003-06:103).
    - Fix: define each in plain words at first use.

20. **L-006-02:109–117.** The impedance-ratio worked example uses unrealistic values (60 Ω cable, "240 Ω folded antenna").
    - Fix: use realistic pairs such as 200 Ω to 50 Ω (4:1) or 450 Ω to 50 Ω (9:1). State that folded dipoles are about 300 Ω, or use the originality allowlist instead of inventing values.

21. **L-006-05:71.** The trap harmonic drawback (B-006-012-007) is missing from the lesson that covers it.

## 5. Module notes

### Lesson order within each module

- **M6** order is reasonable (feedline → loss/SWR → wavelength → directivity → loops), but dipole content is spread across three lessons. L-006-03 should own dipoles completely (pattern, 73 Ω, length), so L-006-05 can focus on random wire, end-fed half-wave, traps and loops. Velocity factor, ratio-based Z0 and standing waves are the core of the first two lessons, and they are the parts that are missing.
- **M3** order follows bank subsection pairs, not how a learner thinks:
  - L-003-04 puts a Yagi antenna inside a receiver lesson.
  - Power supplies are split between L-003-03 and L-003-07, three lessons apart.
  - Stored-charge safety appears in both L-003-03 and L-003-08, with inconsistent advice.

  A better order would be: transmit/receive blocks (01, 02) → signal quality (05) → controls and digital (06, plus the digital-interface half of 03) → power (the linear-supply half of 03, plus 07) → safety (08, split in two). The Yagi parts would become a recap pointing back to L-006-04.

### Gaps and overlaps between modules

- **Yagi parts** (B-003-009) re-teach L-006-04 from scratch.
- **SWR and tuners** (L-003-01) assume L-006-02 but don't say so.
- **Safety Code 6 and RF exposure** (L-003-08) overlap with M1's B-001-024, which comes last in the course. Add a forward pointer.
- **Harmonics** are defined inline in L-003-01, ahead of M8. That's fine.

### Prerequisites from other modules that the front matter should declare

- **M6 needs M5/M4:** impedance and reactance (loading coil "tunes out capacitive reactance"; the tuner "adds reactance"), parallel LC resonance (traps), and decibels (3 dB = ×2, dBi − 2.1 = dBd, stacking).
- **M6 also needs a little M7:** ground wave, for B-006-007-010. Either define it in L-006-03 or flag it as a forward reference.
- **M3 needs:** M5 Ohm's law and power (supply, wire-drop and regulator-heat examples), M4 diodes, capacitors and transformers (linear supply), and M6 SWR and tuners (L-003-01).

### Systemic recommendation

Two patterns across M6 suggest the originality checker is distorting the content:
- unrealistic example numbers (60 Ω cable, 240 Ω folded dipole);
- facts deliberately withheld (0.2 λ at L-006-04:72).

It would be better to state bank facts as principles and use the reviewed allowlist the README describes, rather than teach around the answer.
