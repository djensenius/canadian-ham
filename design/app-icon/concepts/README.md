# App icon concept round 2: filled sumi-e ham antennas

Five exploratory raster concepts are generated by `render_concepts.py`. The generator keeps all output in `design/app-icon/concepts/` and does not modify the approved source/export asset pipeline.

Round 2 replaces the round-1 hairline bristle technique. Each mark is now a single solid filled ink body made by offsetting a centreline with a pressure/width profile, then cutting dry-brush hihaku streaks through the tail. Main strokes are roughly 45-90 px wide at 1024 px, secondary strokes are roughly 25-45 px, with pressed landing blobs, irregular low-frequency edges, a soft bleed halo, and a few deterministic splatter dots on the existing warm cream background.

## Concepts

1. **Yagi beam:** mast, boom, and four short elements; no broadcast arcs.
2. **Radio tower:** narrow two-leg tower with three quick cross strokes and a compact Yagi on top, avoiding hydro-pylon crossarms.
3. **Ground-plane vertical:** one tall radiator, three sloping radials, and a single light ink-wash stroke for depth.
4. **Ensō Yagi:** a single open dry-brush ensō framing a small Yagi-on-mast.
5. **Hex beam:** a mast with six spreader strokes and light ink-wash wire strokes tracing an upturned-umbrella hex beam silhouette.

## Texture test

Before rebuilding the concepts, I rendered one full-size 1024 px stroke with the new filled-stroke construction and opened it at full size. **Critique:** the first texture pass already read as ink from a brush rather than pencil wire: a dense opaque core, pressed landing, irregular edge, and multiple dry cutouts in the final third broke the tail into bristle tips. The one adjustment was to switch the hihaku from painted cream overlays to even-odd cutouts so the streaks are true gaps in the ink body.

## Render-then-critique passes

1. **Pass 1:** Opened both `comparison.png` and `closeups.png`. Brush read: yes, the closeups showed solid ink bodies with hihaku cuts rather than many thin transparent polylines. Podcast-like: no concentric signal arcs. Readable at 60 px: Yagi, tower, and ensō read, but the ground-plane radiator looked like a blunt vertical block and the Yagi mark was slightly undersized for the tile.
2. **Pass 2:** Enlarged the Yagi beam and reversed the ground-plane radiator so the base is pressed and the top lifts into a dry whip. Opened both sheets again. Brush read: stronger, with the ground-plane now showing a real dry lift-off. Podcast-like: still no signal arcs. Readable at 60 px: all four concepts were identifiable; the ground-plane radials were still a little small.
3. **Pass 3:** Extended and thickened the three ground-plane radials, then regenerated `comparison.png` and `closeups.png`. Brush read: yes, the full-resolution crops look painted in a few decisive strokes with solid core ink and even-odd dry tail cutouts. Podcast-like: no; the ensō is a single open brush circle, not broadcast rings. Readable at 60 px: the four silhouettes remain distinct, and at 32 px each still reduces to a recognizable antenna/ensō shape.
4. **Pass 4:** Added the owner-requested `05-hex-beam`, regenerated the sheets as five columns/tiles, and opened both `comparison.png` and `closeups.png`. Brush read: yes, the mast and six spreaders are filled brush bodies with pressed starts and dry tails; the linking wires are translucent filled wash bodies, not hairline tangles. Podcast-like: no concentric arcs. Readable at 60 px: the upturned hexagon umbrella silhouette is clear; at 32 px it simplifies to a small but recognizable hex beam on a mast.
