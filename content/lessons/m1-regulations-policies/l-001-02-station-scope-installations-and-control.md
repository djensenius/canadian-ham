# Station scope, installations, and control

A club member brings a radio to a work day. Before anyone transmits, check four things: both people's authority, the work being done, the message purpose, and who can stop the transmitter.

These checks are separate. Owning a radio does not grant every service or station privilege. Use the qualification-by-privilege matrix here for station work; its companion in L-001-04 adds frequency access and power.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

- check both people's authorization before working on another person's apparatus;
- keep amateur permission separate from other radio services;
- use the qualification matrix to distinguish installation from operation;
- keep messages open, non-commercial and within amateur purposes; and
- assign responsibility while a guest or another operator uses the station.

## Reference facts: Both sides need authority

For work on someone else's apparatus, check your authority and theirs. An amateur certificate does not authorize marine, aeronautical or land-mobile service use.

| Work or service | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Work for someone else using Advanced privileges | Before installing, commissioning, modifying or repairing apparatus, confirm the other person's authorization for it. | Your qualification does not supply their permission. |
| Another person's amateur transmitter | Both people need amateur certificates for installation, repair or placing it in service. | Equipment work still has two authorized sides. |
| Basic plus Morse installing an amateur station for someone else | The recipient needs a valid amateur certificate. | Morse does not remove the other person's duty. |
| Reprogramming land-mobile equipment for another person's 2 m amateur use | The recipient needs an amateur certificate. | Check the service in which the equipment will be used. |
| Normal amateur contacts | Communicate with stations holding similar authority. | The amateur service has its own scope. |
| A 2 W amateur station | A certificate holder with a call sign must supervise it. | Low power does not replace authorization. |
| Licence-exempt equipment | Do not amplify its output with amateur equipment. | Licence-exempt permission has its own conditions. |
| Amateur equipment outside amateur bands | Amateur equipment, modified or not, lacks certification for other bands and must not transmit there. | A frequency setting is not a service authorization. |
| Marine, aeronautical and land-mobile use | Obtain the applicable service authorization. | Amateur authority is not multi-service authority. |
| A radio programmed for both amateur and land-mobile channels | Land-mobile use requires equipment certified and licensed for that service. | Memories in one box do not merge permissions. |

## Reference facts: Qualification-by-privilege matrix for station work

Advanced adds particular station responsibilities; Honours and Morse do not. Keep installing a single-band voice repeater separate from operating a cross-band repeater. A cross-band repeater receives on one band and retransmits on another.

In this matrix, "special work" means installing a single-band voice repeater or club station; installing or operating transmitters or RF amplifiers lacking both professional design and commercial manufacture for amateur use; or remotely changing transmitter frequency, mode or output power.

| Qualifications held | Assemble a commercial transmitter kit of professional design; operate a cross-band repeater | Special work defined above | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic only | Permitted. | Requires Advanced as well. | Basic covers these designed-kit and cross-band activities. |
| Basic with Honours | Permitted. | Requires Advanced as well. | Honours adds frequency access, not these station-work privileges. |
| Basic plus Morse | Permitted. | Requires Advanced as well. | Morse adds frequency access, not these station-work privileges. |
| Basic plus Advanced | Permitted. | Permitted. | Advanced adds the listed installation, equipment and remote-control work. |

| Other station scope | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Location in Canada | A certificate holder may operate across Canada, within their privileges and other applicable rules. | Amateur permission is not limited to the home province. |
| Beacon | May send one-way signals. A beacon identifies itself or supports propagation checks. | A one-way amateur purpose differs from a public broadcast. |

The companion matrix in L-001-04 uses the same qualification rows for band access and power. Neither matrix replaces service, message, supervision or special-band checks.

## Reference facts: Messages stay open and non-commercial

Check purpose and openness separately. A published digital method makes the encoding open; it does not turn business traffic into amateur traffic.

| Message or signal | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Club-net plans | Recreation, code practice and emergency planning fit amateur discussion. | These serve amateur and public-service purposes. |
| Business traffic | Business planning and other business communications are prohibited, including occasional use. | Amateur service is non-commercial. |
| Ordinary public broadcasting | Do not broadcast to the general public. | Amateur communication is not a broadcasting service. |
| Allowed one-way uses | Model-control commands, short station-adjustment signals and Morse practice are permitted. | They serve a defined amateur purpose. |
| Q codes, abbreviations and procedure signals | Use them with meanings that are open, not secret. | Short signals can still be understood publicly. |
| A new digital encoding method | Publish it in the public domain before using it on amateur bands. | Others must be able to learn its meaning. |
| Secret or unpublished encoding | Do not use it. | Amateur messages must have open meaning. |
| Broadcast programming and commercial recordings | Do not retransmit them. | They are not amateur message content. |
| Background audio | Turn its volume down so music or non-amateur signals do not enter the microphone. | The microphone can retransmit unintended content. |

L-001-03 explains the narrow safety-of-life broadcasting exception. It does not open routine broadcasting or commercial traffic.

## Reference facts: Control stays with responsible people

The control point is where the operator can control or stop the transmitter. A transmitting station always needs a qualified control operator there. The owner remains responsible too.

| Person or situation | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Owner and control operator | Both share responsibility for compliant station operation, including a visitor using someone else's station. | Lending the controls does not remove the owner's duty. |
| Choosing the control operator | The owner may choose any suitably qualified amateur. | Qualifications must fit the operation. |
| While transmitting | The control operator must be at the control point. | Someone must be able to change or stop the signal. |
| A guest without a certificate | May operate only with an appropriately qualified certificate holder present and supervising. | Participation is not independent control. |
| An unqualified family member alone | Cannot act as control operator; suitable amateur qualifications are needed. | Family access to equipment is not operating authority. |

## How these rules fit together

Use the same order at a home station and a club work day. Check authority for the apparatus and service. Then check the activity against the station-work matrix. Next check what the signal will carry. Finally assign control and supervision.

These checks cannot substitute for each other. Advanced does not authorize another service. Open encoding does not authorize business content. Ownership does not qualify a person as control operator, and a supervised guest does not take away the owner's responsibility.

## Radio connection

### In the real world: a club work day

A donor may own the radio, a volunteer may bring the tools, and someone else may test into a dummy load. Assign the qualified people before starting, not after the first transmission.

Label multi-service channel banks clearly and check the selected channel before keying. Keep background audio low. Put the person who can stop the transmitter at the control point.

## Summary

Think authority, activity, purpose, then control. The qualification matrix answers only the activity check. A lawful transmission also needs the right service authority, amateur message content and responsible people at the controls.
