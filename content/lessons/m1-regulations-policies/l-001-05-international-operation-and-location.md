# International, third-party, and station location rules

Amateur radio crosses borders, but permission does not travel in a loose way. A Canadian operator has to consider who is communicating, the message purpose, the place where the station is operating, the applicable visit arrangement, and the required identification pattern.

Use these rows as exam facts. Before a real trip, maritime operation, foreign portable plan, or third-party traffic plan, check current official sources for the destination because operating rules can change.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

- explain Canadian third-party and visiting-operator communication limits;
- keep international amateur messages within amateur-purpose or personal content;
- identify the ITU regulatory role behind international amateur operation;
- use ITU regions as map references tied to the operating authority actually in force;
- distinguish United States and CEPT-style visit requirements; and
- explain why operating location matters for bands, limits, and identification.

## Reference facts: Third-party and visiting-operator communication

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Third-party communications | Conducted on behalf of a person without amateur radio certification. |
| Third-party content in the Canadian amateur service | Personal and non-commercial only. |
| Canadian third-party communications | May be provided with any other amateur radio station. |
| Canadian international third-party traffic | Canada permits international communications on behalf of third parties. |
| International emergency or disaster-relief third-party traffic | Expressly permitted; a specific prohibition by the foreign administration concerned is the only bar. |
| Foreign administration objection to communication | A country can preclude communication by filing an objection with the ITU. |
| Foreign visitors other than United States citizens | Their country must have an agreement with Canada and the operator must have the appropriate permit. |
| CEPT amateur licence holders operating in Canada | Granted Advanced privileges by ISED. |
| United States-issued call sign holder operating in Canada | Must indicate location by city and province at least once during a contact. |
| United States-issued call sign holder in Canada using voice | Identifies with the call sign, mobile or portable qualifier, and the Canadian prefix for the station's geographic location. |
| Supervising a non-certified guest during a contact | Keep supervising and monitoring the guest, including if a foreign station joins, because you remain responsible for the station and international third-party traffic depends on the foreign administration not objecting. |
| Local non-certified participants at both ends | Both non-certified people are third parties. |

## Reference facts: International message purpose and regulatory source

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |

## Reference facts: ITU regions and operating location

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Canada | ITU Region 2. |
| Europe and Africa | ITU Region 1. |
| Australia, Japan, and Southeast Asia | ITU Region 3. |
| Canadian operator in Florida | Subject to frequency band limits applicable to United States amateur radio operators. |
| Canadian operator seven kilometres offshore from Florida | Subject to frequency band limits applicable to United States amateur radio operators because 7 km is inside the United States territorial sea. |

## Reference facts: United States and CEPT-style visits

| Visit situation | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Canadian operator in the United States | Basic is the minimum Canadian qualification for this visit arrangement. |
| Canadian operator in the United States using voice | Transmit the Canadian call sign, then portable or mobile as appropriate, then the prefix for the US call area being visited. |
| Canadian operator using a CEPT Amateur Radio Licence in a participating country | Advanced is the minimum Canadian qualification for this visit arrangement. |
| CEPT participating country using voice | Transmit the visited country's prefix, then "stroke," then the Canadian call sign. |

## How these rules fit together

Third-party and visiting-operator rows ask two questions. Who is speaking or sending the message, and whose operating permission is being used? Ask those before sending the traffic.

A Canadian certified operator remains responsible while supervising a non-certified person at the station. That responsibility includes monitoring the contact when a foreign station joins. International third-party communication also depends on whether the foreign administration has objected.

International communication starts with purpose. Amateur-purpose messages and personal remarks fit the service. The ITU row adds the international regulatory source and the administration-objection rule.

Region rows are map references. Region 2 includes the Americas, while Region 1 includes Europe and Africa and Region 3 includes Australia, Japan, and Southeast Asia. These labels help orient the operator, but they do not replace the operating authority for the exact location. The United States rows show that location can make another administration's band limits the relevant limits.

Seven kilometres offshore from Florida is about 3.8 nautical miles. The United States territorial sea extends to 12 nautical miles, about 22 km, so United States amateur limits still govern in that example.

The United States and CEPT rows are easy to reverse because both involve a Canadian operator outside Canada. In the United States, your home call comes first, then portable or mobile, then the local area prefix. Under CEPT, the host country's prefix comes first, then stroke, then the Canadian call sign.

Morse-code authorization also belongs to the administration framework. Learn who decides the Morse requirement as an administration fact.

### Memory trick: home call or host prefix first

- Helps remember: United States and CEPT voice-identification order.
- Trick: in the United States visit row, start with your Canadian home call; in the CEPT row, start with the host country's prefix.
- Limit: this is only a study cue. Always check the current official visit rules before a real trip.

## Radio connection

### In the real world: portable habits while travelling

A travel station can move faster than memory. Before transmitting, write the current location, visit arrangement, band limits, identification pattern, and third-party limits on the same operating note card.

Keep the note card with the radio, headset, power lead, and portable antenna. That ties the rule check to the hardware you will actually use.

A clean signal, a good antenna, and a friendly contact still need matching message purpose, destination permission, supervision, and location-based limits.

## Summary

- Third-party communication depends on who the message is for and who is responsible for the station.
- International amateur communication stays within amateur-purpose or personal messages.
- Another administration's objection belongs in the international permission check.
- Canadian operators must consider ITU radio regulations as well as Canadian requirements.
- ITU regions serve as map references within a larger destination-permission check.
- Travel arrangements pair Canadian qualification rows with location-specific voice-identification orders.
