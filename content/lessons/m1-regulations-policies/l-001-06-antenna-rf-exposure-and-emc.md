# Antenna consultation, RF exposure, and EMC

Antenna projects are visible, RF fields are invisible, and interference complaints can become emotional. The exam groups these topics because a responsible station owner has to consider neighbours, land-use authorities, people near antennas, and affected electronic equipment.

This lesson states only facts established by the official answers. Use it to pass the exam and to build careful habits, then consult current ISED and Health Canada material before a real installation or RF exposure evaluation.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

- identify antenna-siting authority, public consultation paths, exclusions, and concern-handling duties;
- distinguish municipal process details from ISED default process and final decision authority;
- describe the named human RF-exposure framework without inventing a safe-wattage shortcut;
- name the RF exposure factors and quantities identified by the official bank; and
- apply EMCAB-2 field-strength and equipment-category facts for interference complaints.

## Reference facts: Antenna siting and public consultation

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Installation or modification of an antenna structure | The bank rejects erecting any size structure without consulting neighbours or the local land-use authority. |
| Antenna installations, including masts and towers | Authority rests with the Minister of Innovation, Science and Industry. |
| ISED-defined exclusion criterion | The operator may not be required to contact land-use authorities to determine public consultation requirements. |
| No local land-use approval process | Follow ISED's default public consultation process. |
| Element NOT identified in ISED's public consultation process | Participating in public meetings on the project. |
| ISED default-process concerns | Address reasonable and relevant concerns provided in writing within the 30-day public comment period. |
| Municipality with a consultation process | Public consultation might not be required when the system is excluded by the municipal process OR by CPC-2-0-03. |
| Proponent and non-public stakeholder fail to agree | ISED makes the final decision. |
| Tallest structure without public consultation | The tallest exempted by the land-use authority or ISED. |
| Local process exists | The municipality or local land-use authority determines how public consultation should take place. |

## Reference facts: Safety Code 6 and RF exposure

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Organization publishing guidelines for maximum RF energy near the human body | Health Canada. |
| Purpose of Safety Code 6 | Gives RF exposure limits for the human body. |
| Greatest RF-energy risk range | 48 MHz to 300 MHz. |
| Reason for the lowest exposure limit in that range | The human body absorbs RF energy the most in this range. |
| Maximum safe power output to the antenna of a hand-held VHF or UHF radio | Not specified. |
| Basic exposure situations | Controlled and uncontrolled environments. |
| Other established adverse effect below 10 MHz | Nerve stimulation. |
| Hand-held transmitters | Not excluded from Safety Code 6 requirements. |
| Transmitter exemptions | No transmitters are exempt. |
| Exposure quantities | Safety Code 6 is not stated simply as power fed into antennas; the official answer set points to fields, currents, and absorption rate. |
| Exposure factors identified by the bank | Antenna gain, distance, transmitter power, and frequency. |

## Reference facts: EMCAB-2 interference criteria

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Stereo-system complaint | Lack of immunity is the cause when field strength at the neighbour's premises is below ISED's specified immunity criteria. |
| Television-receiver complaint | The amateur transmission is the cause when field strength at the neighbour's premises is above ISED's specified immunity criteria. |
| Home-entertainment devices that are not strictly radio apparatus | Associated equipment. |
| Garage door opener affected by transmissions | Radio-sensitive equipment. |

## How these rules fit together

Antenna-siting rows describe process. They do not create one universal height rule, and they do not say every file follows the same path. The station owner first looks for the applicable local process or exclusion. If there is no local approval process, the ISED default process is the relevant path. Written concerns in the default process are handled according to the timeliness and relevance row, and unresolved non-public stakeholder disputes go to ISED for a final decision.

The exposure-code rows describe human RF exposure. They name Health Canada, the purpose of the code, the exposure situations, the frequency-related effects, and the fact that no transmitter type is simply exempt. Transmitter power matters, but it is one factor among gain, distance, and frequency, and the exposure quantities are broader than watts fed into an antenna.

EMCAB-2 rows describe interference cause decisions for affected equipment. The important comparison is field strength at the affected premises against ISED's specified immunity criterion, along with the equipment category. Operating within amateur power privileges is not the same question as whether an affected device meets the EMCAB-2 criterion.

## Radio connection

These rules connect directly to station design. A tidy antenna installation is easier to discuss with neighbours. A station layout that keeps people away from radiating elements is easier to operate responsibly. A clean transmitter, good feedline, and proper filtering help, but an EMCAB-2 question still turns on field strength at the affected premises and the relevant equipment category.

Good operators document. Keep dates, contact names, notices, equipment settings used during complaint investigation, and changes made to the station. Documentation does not replace the rule, but it helps everyone understand what was checked and why.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming an amateur antenna project is exempt from consultation because it is non-commercial.
- Memorizing a single antenna height as a universal no-consultation limit.
- Turning an OR exclusion row into an AND requirement.
- Treating transmitter power as the only RF exposure quantity.
- Assuming hand-held or low-power transmitters are automatically outside RF exposure rules.
- Deciding an EMCAB-2 complaint from on/off symptoms instead of field strength at the affected premises.
- Confusing associated equipment with radio-sensitive equipment.

## Summary

- Antenna installation and modification can involve ISED procedures, local land-use processes, exclusions, and responsible handling of community concerns.
- The Minister of Innovation, Science and Industry is the authority named for antenna installations, while a local process can determine how consultation takes place.
- Health Canada is named for human RF-exposure guidelines, and the named code sets the human-exposure limit framework.
- The official exposure factors include antenna gain, distance, transmitter power, and frequency; no transmitter type is treated as exempt in the bank.
- Exposure rules are about exposure quantities, not merely watts into an antenna.
- EMCAB-2 cause decisions use field strength at the affected premises relative to ISED immunity criteria, plus the relevant equipment category.
