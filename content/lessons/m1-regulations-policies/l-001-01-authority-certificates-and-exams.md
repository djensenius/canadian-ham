# Authority, certificates, and exams

Canadian amateur radio is a licensed radio service. The rules in this lesson tell you who sets the rules, what your certificate record means, and how exams and eligibility work.

Treat the tables as reference facts. The prose explains how the rows connect so the official quiz questions feel less like isolated memory work.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

- match the Radiocommunication Act, the Radiocommunication Regulations, ISED, and operating standards to their roles;
- keep certificate validity, address, inspection, and call-sign-fee facts separate;
- explain why false information, false distress traffic, unlawful obstruction, and due process belong with legal authority; and
- state the Basic-entry, later-qualification, fee, and accommodation facts with their supported conditions; and
- prepare for exam-day material rules, including the allowed calculator and reference sheet.

## Reference facts: Authority and source roles

| Source or record | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Radiocommunication Act | Authority to make Canadian radiocommunication regulations; authority to publish amateur-station operating standards; source of radiocommunication offences and penalties. |
| Radiocommunication Regulations | Define the Canadian amateur radio service. |
| Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) | Administers the Radiocommunication Act. |
| ISED operating standards | Provide operating standards for amateur stations. |
| Certificate and call sign | Identify the holder's qualifications and station identity for amateur operation. |

## Reference facts: Certificate records, addresses, and call-sign fees

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Certificate validity | Life. |
| Certificate retention | Retained at the station and at the address provided to ISED. |
| Inspector request | The holder has 48 hours to show the certificate or a copy. |
| Mailing-address change | ISED must be informed within 30 days of a mailing-address change. |
| First Amateur Radio Operator Certificate | Free. |
| Replacement call sign with a new prefix after moving to a new province or territory | Free. |
| Voluntary call-sign change, including a two-letter call sign | $60. |

## Reference facts: Offences, penalties, and process boundaries

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Transmitting outside amateur bands | Prohibited; penalties could be assessed to the control operator. |
| False distress wording when there is no emergency | A false or fraudulent message. |
| Transmitting false information | Prohibited. |
| False or fraudulent distress signal or message | Fine up to $5 000, prison term up to one year, or both. |
| Interfering with or obstructing radio communication without lawful cause | Fine up to $5 000, prison term up to one year, or both. |
| Dwelling entry by inspector | Consent or a warrant is required for dwelling entry. |
| Inspection warrant safeguards | When entry is refused and entry is needed for inspector duties, the inspector may obtain a warrant; warrant-related force requires authorization and a peace officer. |
| Certificate suspension | Notice and an opportunity to make representations are part of the suspension process. |

## Reference facts: Basic eligibility, qualifications, exams, and accommodations

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Basic age | No age limit. |
| Candidate address | A valid address in Canada is required. |
| First amateur examination sequence | Basic must be passed before the Amateur Radio Operator Certificate is issued. |
| Later amateur qualifications | After Basic is issued, additional qualifications may be examined in any order. |
| Morse qualification | 5 words per minute sending and receiving. |
| Other radio services | Operation in another radio service requires authority for that service. |
| Named bridge credential | The Canadian Radiocommunication Operator General Certificate Maritime (RGMC) can lead to amateur certification. |
| ISED office qualification examinations | No charge. |
| Accredited examiner | May charge a fee to recover examination administration costs. |
| Accredited-examiner qualifications | Basic, Advanced, and Morse code qualifications. |
| Disability-related examination delivery | May be oral or tailored to the candidate's ability to complete the examination. |
| Morse sending accommodation | A disabled candidate may be allowed to recite the examination text in Morse code sounds. |
| Required examination portions | All required portions of the examination remain part of an accommodated exam. |
| Reading difficulty or language barrier | Oral examination delivery may be available. |

## Reference facts: Exam day materials and virtual exams

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Closed book | The Basic exam is closed book. Do not use books, notes, lessons, web pages, or the app during the exam. |
| Permitted reference sheet | The only reference material named by ISED for the Basic exam is the ISED-approved formula and block diagram sheet labelled "Reference Material for Amateur Radio Exam – Basic Qualification". |
| Version to practise with | Use the unlabelled exam version of the sheet when practising. It is in ISED's reference-material ZIP on the Amateur Radio Operator Certificate Services downloads page: https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/amateur-radio-operator-certificate-services/en/downloads |
| Calculator | A calculator is permitted only if it cannot store formulas or text. |
| Scrap paper | RIC-1 does not give a general scrap-paper permission. Follow your examiner's instructions. |
| In-person exam preparation | Ask the examiner what identification and printed material they want you to bring. Keep everything else away from the exam desk. |
| Virtual exam setup | Use a computer with a camera, microphone, and stable internet connection. Have valid government-issued photo identification ready, and be ready to show that nothing within reach or view can affect exam integrity. |
| Virtual answer sheet | For a virtual exam, the examiner emails a blank answer sheet in advance. Print it, write answers on it during the exam, and follow the examiner's submission instructions. |
| App practice match | Use mock exams as exam-condition practice: one question from each subsection, no lesson notes inside the exam, optional timer off by default, and only the allowed calculator and ISED sheet beside you when you choose a realistic run. |

## How these rules fit together

The legal-source rows are the top layer. The Act gives authority, the Regulations define the amateur service, ISED administers the Act, and the operating standards tell amateur stations how to operate.

Certificate rows combine two ideas. Your certificate lasts for your lifetime, but record duties continue. Keep the station copy, keep the address current, and be ready to show the certificate or a copy when a radio inspector properly asks.

Offence rows protect trust in radio. False information, false distress traffic, and unlawful obstruction can harm people and services. The same group also includes process protections, such as the limits on dwelling entry and the suspension process.

Exam rows describe a path. Basic is the first amateur qualification. After the certificate is issued, later qualifications can be examined in any order. Accommodation rows change delivery, not the required examination content.

Exam-day material rows describe the desk, not the study plan. Study with lessons, notes, and the labelled training sheet before the exam. During the exam, switch to closed-book conditions: no notes, no lesson pages, no app, an allowed calculator, and the unlabelled ISED exam sheet.

The reference sheet helps with formulas and block diagrams. It does not replace understanding. Practise with the unlabelled version so the real sheet feels familiar without hidden labels.

Virtual exams add an identification and room check. The examiner needs to see your valid government-issued photo identification and your workspace. Keep only the allowed materials within reach, print the emailed answer sheet before the exam, and leave the camera and microphone on as instructed.

### Memory trick: life record, address-change notice

- Helps remember: certificate validity and the mailing-address duty are different facts.
- Trick: think "life for the certificate, 30 days to report a mailing-address change." The same number, 30, also appears in station identification (minutes between IDs).
- Limit: this is a memory cue only. Use the table when you need the exact deadline in days.

## Radio connection

### In the real world: the station binder

Many operators keep a small station binder or folder. It can hold the certificate copy, current call sign information, the station address, equipment manuals, and notes from any examiner or ISED contact.

The folder is not a magic legal shield. It is a simple habit that keeps the lifetime certificate, address record, inspection duty, and exam path in one place.

Add an exam-day checklist to the folder. Include examiner contact details, identification instructions, calculator check, and the ISED downloads page for the unlabelled Basic reference sheet.

During emergency-support practice, these facts reduce confusion. Operators who separate real distress traffic from drills and keep current certificate records are easier to trust when conditions are stressful.

## Summary

- The Act, Regulations, ISED, and operating standards have different roles.
- Certificate records combine continuing validity with continuing administrative duties.
- False information, false distress, and unlawful obstruction belong with legal consequences and public trust.
- The certification path links entry eligibility, first certification, later qualifications, and service boundaries.
- Exam fees and accommodations depend on the exact setting while preserving the required examination content.
- Exam day is closed book, with only an allowed calculator and the ISED-approved Basic reference sheet as permitted materials.
