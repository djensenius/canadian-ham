# Authority, certificates, and exams

Canadian amateur radio is a licensed radio service. The rules in this lesson tell you where Canadian amateur authority comes from, how your certificate record is handled, and which exam and eligibility facts the current Basic bank uses. Treat these items as reference material: the lesson explains how the rows relate, and the quiz asks only official bank questions.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

- match the Radiocommunication Act, the Radiocommunication Regulations, ISED, and operating standards to their roles;
- keep certificate validity, address, inspection, and call-sign-fee facts separate;
- describe why false information, false distress traffic, unlawful obstruction, and due process are grouped with legal authority; and
- state the Basic-entry, later-qualification, fee, and accommodation facts without adding unsupported conditions.

## Reference facts: Authority and source roles

| Source or record | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Radiocommunication Act | Authority to make Canadian radiocommunication regulations; authority to publish amateur-station operating standards; source of radiocommunication offences and penalties. |
| Radiocommunication Regulations | Define the Canadian amateur radio service. |
| Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) | Administers the Radiocommunication Act. |
| ISED operating standards | Provide operating standards for amateur stations. |
| Certificate and call sign | Identify the holder's qualifications and station identity for amateur operation. |

## Reference facts: Certificate records, addresses, and call-sign fees

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Certificate validity | Life. |
| Certificate retention | Retained at the station and at the address provided to ISED. |
| Inspector request | The holder has 48 hours to show the certificate or a copy. |
| Mailing-address change | ISED must be informed within 30 days of a mailing-address change. |
| First Amateur Radio Operator Certificate | Free. |
| Replacement call sign with a new prefix after moving to a new province or territory | Free. |
| Voluntary call-sign change, including a two-letter call sign | $60. |

## Reference facts: Offences, penalties, and process boundaries

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Transmitting outside amateur bands | Prohibited; penalties could be assessed to the control operator. |
| False distress wording when there is no emergency | A false or fraudulent message. |
| Transmitting false information | Never permitted. |
| False or fraudulent distress signal or message | Fine up to $5,000, prison term up to one year, or both. |
| Interfering with or obstructing radio communication without lawful cause | Fine up to $5,000, prison term up to one year, or both. |
| Dwelling entry by inspector | The bank rejects entry without consent and without a warrant. |
| Certificate suspension | The bank rejects suspension with no notice or opportunity to make representations. |

## Reference facts: Basic eligibility, qualifications, exams, and accommodations

| Topic | Fact established for this lesson |
|---|---|
| Basic age | No age limit. |
| Candidate address | A valid address in Canada is required. |
| First amateur examination sequence | Basic must be passed before the Amateur Radio Operator Certificate is issued. |
| Later amateur qualifications | After Basic is issued, additional qualifications may be examined in any order. |
| Morse qualification | 5 words per minute sending and receiving. |
| Other radio services | Amateur Advanced qualification does not authorize operation in another radio service. |
| Named bridge credential | The Canadian Radiocommunication Operator General Certificate Maritime (RGMC) can lead to amateur certification. |
| ISED office qualification examinations | No charge. |
| Accredited examiner | May charge a fee to recover examination administration costs. |
| Accredited-examiner qualifications | Basic, Advanced, and Morse code qualifications. |
| Disability-related examination delivery | May be oral or tailored to the candidate's ability to complete the examination. |
| Morse sending accommodation | A disabled candidate may be allowed to recite the examination text in Morse code sounds. |
| Required examination portions | Accommodation does not remove required portions of the examination. |
| Reading difficulty or not usually speaking English or French | The bank supports an oral-examination possibility. |
| Interpreter for insufficient English or French | The bank identifies interpreter accompaniment as the NOT-correct statement. |
| Medical evidence | An examiner may request evidence from a practising medical physician before accommodating testing. |

## How these rules fit together

The legal-source rows explain why the rest of the lesson matters: amateur operating privileges come from Canadian radiocommunication law, not from club custom or equipment capability. Use the Act, Regulations, ISED, and operating standards as separate layers rather than as interchangeable document names.

Certificate records have two ideas at the same time. The certificate itself does not expire on a yearly schedule, but the holder still has record duties. Address updates, retention, and inspector requests are administrative duties that continue during the life of the certificate. Fee rows also depend on the exact service being requested, so do not merge first certificates, address-related prefix replacements, and voluntary call-sign changes.

The offence rows connect truthful operation with public trust. False information and false distress traffic are not merely poor operating style; the bank ties them to legal consequences. The same section also preserves due-process limits, so enforcement authority and individual process protections should be learned together.

The Basic-entry rows build the certification path. Basic is the first amateur qualification for the certificate, later qualifications follow after that certificate is issued, and an amateur qualification stays within the amateur service. Examination accommodation rows then explain delivery support without changing the required content of the exam.

## Radio connection

A clean station is not only an RF-clean station. It is also a station whose operator knows which authority applies, where the certificate record belongs, when ISED must be told about an address change, and when a message or channel choice leaves amateur authority.

During emergency-support practice, these facts reduce confusion. Operators who separate real distress traffic from drills, and who avoid invented record or qualification rules, are easier to trust when conditions are stressful.

## Common mistakes

- Treating legal-source names as interchangeable labels.
- Assuming lifetime certificate validity removes address, retention, or inspection duties.
- Moving a fee or deadline from one certificate service to another.
- Treating false information as acceptable because the setting is a drill or a club activity.
- Generalizing the named RGMC bridge to unrelated certificates.
- Using an amateur qualification as if it authorized another radio service.
- Confusing supported examination delivery changes with removal of required exam content.
- Adding regulatory numbers or conditions from memory instead of using the current official bank and verified project facts.

## Summary

- The Act, Regulations, ISED, and operating standards have different roles.
- The certificate is valid for life, but address, retention, inspection, and fee facts still matter.
- False information, false distress, and unlawful obstruction are legal issues, not only etiquette problems.
- Basic has no age limit, requires a valid Canadian address, and comes before later amateur qualifications.
- Exam fees and accommodations depend on the exact setting and do not change the required examination content.
