# Authority, certificates, and exams

Before you key a transmitter, you need authority to operate it. Start with three principles: named documents set the rules, qualifications define your permission, and responsibility continues after certification.

The tables keep exact rules close to their purpose. For review, cover the Rule and Why columns, recall the rule from the left-hand label, then uncover both columns. The M1 numbers board near the end keeps similar-looking numbers attached to their different jobs.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

- find the source of an operating rule;
- describe the path from Basic to later qualifications;
- keep your certificate and address records ready for inspection;
- explain why honest signals and fair inspection procedures matter; and
- prepare an exam desk with only permitted aids.

## Reference facts: Authority comes from named sources

The Act grants legal powers. Regulations and technical standards serve different jobs under those powers; they are not steps in a ladder of qualifications.

| Source | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Radiocommunication Act | Grants powers to make regulations and issue station standards; sets offences and penalties. | Start here for the legal authority. |
| Radiocommunication Regulations | Define the amateur radio service in Canada. | A service definition sets the scope of the permission. |
| Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) | Administers the Act. | The department carries out the federal radio program. |
| RBR-4 | Gives the technical operating standards for amateur stations. | Use it for how the station may operate. |
| Certificate and call sign | Record the operator's qualifications and the station's identity. | Permission and identification work together. |

## Reference facts: Qualifications follow a learning path

Basic comes first. Once the certificate is issued, later qualifications can follow in any order. Exam accommodations change how a candidate demonstrates knowledge, not which required parts count.

| Part of the path | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Starting Basic | Any age is eligible; the candidate needs a valid Canadian address. | Age and address are separate entry facts. |
| First certificate | Pass Basic before the amateur certificate is issued. | Basic is the foundation for later qualifications. |
| Adding qualifications | After Basic certification, take later qualification exams in any order. | There is no fixed sequence after Basic. |
| Morse qualification | Demonstrate sending and receiving at 5 words per minute. | Both directions are tested. |
| Maritime certificate route | The Canadian Radiocommunication Operator General Certificate Maritime (RGMC) can lead to amateur certification. | Remember this named certificate route separately. |
| Another radio service | Obtain that service's authority, even if you hold Advanced. | Amateur qualifications do not authorize other services. |
| ISED office exams | Qualification exams carry no charge. | Distinguish the venue from the qualification. |
| Accredited examiner fees | The examiner may recover exam administration costs. | This is not an ISED qualification fee. |
| Accredited examiner qualifications | The examiner holds Basic, Advanced and Morse. | The examiner needs the full qualification set. |
| Disability accommodations | Oral or tailored delivery may be available, including exam segments. All required parts still apply. | Access can change without removing the assessment. |
| Morse sending accommodation | A disabled candidate may be allowed to speak the Morse sounds of the exam text. | This changes the sending method. |
| Medical evidence | The examiner may ask for evidence from a practising physician before adapting the test. | Arrange accommodations with the examiner. |
| Reading or language difficulty | Oral delivery may follow a written failure caused by reading difficulty or by English or French not being the candidate's usual language. | Keep the prior-failure condition with this provision. |
| Language of answers | The candidate answers personally in English or French; oral delivery is the language aid. | The candidate still demonstrates their own knowledge. |

## Reference facts: A lifetime certificate needs current records

The certificate lasts, but the record must stay useful. Keep the certificate at the station and at the address held by ISED. A move changes your record duties, not your certificate's lifetime validity.

| Record duty | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Validity | The certificate remains valid for the holder's lifetime. | Renewal is not the same issue as updating an address. |
| Keeping the certificate | Retain it at the station, at the address supplied to ISED. | Keep the record available where required. |
| Inspection request | Produce the certificate or a copy within 48 hours. | An inspector must be able to verify qualifications. |
| Mailing-address change | Notify ISED within 30 days. | A lifetime record still needs current contact details. |
| First certificate | Issued free of charge. | Separate certificate cost from an examiner's costs. |
| Moving province or territory | A replacement call sign with the new prefix is free. | This change follows the move. |
| Voluntary call-sign change | Costs $60, including a change to a two-letter call sign. | A chosen change has its own fee. |

## Reference facts: Trust needs honest signals and fair process

A false distress call can divert help from a real emergency. Honest transmissions protect others; limits on inspection and suspension also protect the certificate holder.

| Situation | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Operating outside amateur bands | Outside-band transmissions are prohibited; the control operator can face penalties. | Amateur authority has a defined scope. |
| False information | Do not transmit false information. A distress call without an emergency is a false or fraudulent message. | Other people may act on what they hear. |
| False distress, or unlawful interference or obstruction | Each can bring up to $5 000 in fines, up to a year in prison, or both. | These acts can disrupt communication and help. |
| Entry into a dwelling | The inspector needs consent or a warrant. | A home has specific entry protections. |
| Warrant and force | A warrant may follow refused entry needed for inspection duties. Force needs warrant authorization and a peace officer. | Inspection powers have conditions. |
| Certificate suspension | The process includes notice and a chance to make representations. | The holder has an opportunity to respond. |

## Reference facts: Exam aids support skills, not stored answers

Study with your notes, lessons and app. At the exam, put them away. The permitted sheet supplies formulas and block diagrams, while you supply the understanding.

| Exam preparation | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Closed-book desk | Use no reference material except ISED's approved Basic formula and block diagram sheet. | Study materials are not exam materials. |
| Named sheet | The approved aid is "Reference Material for Amateur Radio Exam – Basic Qualification". | Practise with the unlabelled exam version. |
| Calculator | It must be unable to store text or formulas. | Stored notes are not an allowed aid. |
| Virtual equipment | Prepare a computer, camera, microphone and stable internet. | The examiner must be able to supervise the exam. |
| Virtual answer sheet | Print the blank sheet emailed by the examiner; write on it and follow submission instructions. | Prepare the paper before the session starts. |

ISED provides the exam sheet. For practice, download the reference-material ZIP from https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/amateur-radio-operator-certificate-services/en/downloads and choose the English unlabelled exam version, not the labelled training version.

Follow the examiner's identification instructions and ask about scrap paper in advance. For a virtual exam, be ready to show the room and workspace. Keep only allowed materials within reach and keep the camera and microphone on as instructed.

## How these rules fit together

Permission has a source, a holder and continuing duties. Use the Act to understand the legal powers, the Regulations for service rules, and RBR-4 for technical operation. ISED administers the system; your certificate records your place in it.

Keep exam costs, certificate costs and call-sign costs separate. Also separate access to an exam from its content: an accommodation can change delivery while keeping every required part.

After certification, keep records current and signals honest. A station binder can hold a certificate copy, current address, call-sign details and examiner or ISED correspondence. It supports these habits; it does not replace the rules.

## Radio connection

Before emergency-support practice, check who is qualified and whether the station's records are current. Keep real distress separate from drills. Trustworthy messages and ready records make a station easier to rely on.

Use mock exams to rehearse exam conditions: one official item per subsection, no lesson notes, and the optional timer off unless you choose it. A timer is a practice choice, not an ISED time limit.

## Summary

Permission starts in the legal documents and is recorded in your certificate. It continues through current records and honest transmissions. Learn that path first, then use the numbers board to keep each deadline, fee and operating limit attached to its own job.
